Lootholma is one of those places in Finland that won’t impress people by photos alone, not the way Norwegian fjords or waves crashing against Madeira’s cliffs do. But it always resonates with people looking for peace, silence, and the sea calmly washing the shore.

![Sea shore at Lootholma](/images/kustavi-lootholma-3.jpg "Sea shore at Lootholma")

I stumbled on this place by accident during my summer trip, when I decided to explore the Turku archipelago a little. I only had a couple of hours before heading on to Tampere, so I was mostly just driving around and enjoying the views. Eventually I reached the Sale grocery store and gas station in Kustavi, and looking at the map, I noticed a place just a five-minute drive south.

That place turned out to be [Lootholma](https://lootholma.fi/), a resort and campsite surrounded by the sea. At reception I was told I could park on the grounds for free, which was quite a surprise for someone used to Central and Western Europe. I made myself a coffee with milk and went out to see what was going on.

![Swimming site next to one of the cabins](/images/kustavi-lootholma-5.jpg "Swimming site next to one of the cabins")

As it usually happens in Finland, nothing was going on. Just modern cabins among pine trees, saunas (public hours were, of course, on a different day), a few camper vans, silence, and the sound of wind and sea. Exactly what I was looking for. In fact, it was so exactly what I needed that I forgot to take any pictures. The photos here are from a trip I made later in autumn.

When I came back to reception, I got another taste of Finnish hospitality. A man in his fifties, sipping coffee on the porch, asked where I was from, first in Finnish (all I know is “yksi olut, kiitos”) and then in English. He told me that years ago he and his wife had come here for just one night, finding it almost the same way I did. Now they spend every summer here, and I could clearly see why.

![Wooden cabins on the seashore](/images/kustavi-lootholma-2.jpg "Wooden cabins on the seashore")

The most charming part came at the end of our chat. He grabbed one of the old-school paper maps of the area and marked every place worth visiting in the archipelago. I went to one of them right away, the Jeremiah Caves, and it was definitely worth it, especially if you’re into geological formations. I don’t know who thinks Finns are cold and unapproachable, but so far my experiences have been nothing but positive.

![Paper map with handwritten markings](/images/kustavi-map-1.jpeg "Paper map with handwritten markings")

We shook hands, and I went on to explore the caves. And then it was time for me to leave and explore another region of Finland.
